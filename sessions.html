Session 01

Six Goblins and a Dead Train

The Desert Rose was still two days from Abrams Rest when the windows broke.

Six goblins came through the glass in a storm of teeth, blades and noise. Until that moment, the people sharing the carriage had little reason to trust one another. Chango K Caza had boarded with a prisoner in chains. Coren Vael watched everyone with mismatched eyes and the habits of someone who preferred knowing where the exits were. Hylanku Lonehorn barely spoke. Lira Cervan and Luxem had recognised one another as elves without finding much comfort in the fact. Quercucoonie travelled with something that looked like a tree and moved like a much less patient creature.

Introductions would have to wait.

Chango answered the attack with his pistols. Lira and Luxem used magic despite the confined carriage, the danger and the possibility that somebody might remember seeing it later. Hylanku put his body where the weapons were. Coren moved through the confusion with a blade.

Quercucoonie summoned Verdant Shade.

The eidolon struck one of the goblins once. Nothing further was required.

When the last attacker fell, the carriage went quiet except for the train, the injured and whatever remained of the windows. Lira treated Luxem's wounds with mud. The remedy worked. Its dignity was more questionable, but Luxem accepted it with only the resistance that could still be managed while bleeding.

Engineer Thistogg arrived after the fighting and gave them little time to enjoy surviving.

The other carriages had been attacked as well. Every other passenger aboard the Desert Rose was dead.

The goblins had also stolen the train's magicite core. Without it, the engine could not continue to Abrams Rest. The Desert Rose had survived the Great Devastation and whatever centuries had followed, only to be stranded in the Blighted West by six creatures with more appetite than planning.

Thistogg gave the survivors three possibilities.

Pem lay thirty miles to the west and might offer aid. A temple to the northeast was rumoured to contain raw magicite. Or they could follow the goblins and recover the stolen core directly.

Nathan Indiana Croft, the archaeologist chained to Chango, recommended the temple. He spoke of treasure as though the dead passengers had left the world merely to simplify the division of it. Chango listened without removing the restraints.

The group considered the roads ahead. Pem was distant. The temple was uncertain. The goblins had left tracks.

So the strangers made their first decision together.

They would hunt the thieves.

The Desert Rose waited behind them, silent on its rails. Ahead lay the open Blight, the Rabbit Rock Mountains and whatever had allowed the goblins to return without being seen.

They left the dead train and followed.

Session 02

What Killed the Goblin Camp

Chango declared himself an experienced tracker.

The trail led east.

By sunset the party had covered roughly ten miles, following signs through the barren country. Half-eaten rock rabbits confirmed that goblins had passed that way and suggested the thieves were making for the Rabbit Rock Mountains. It was not elegant work, but the dead rarely object to being used as directions.

The party made camp beneath the open sky.

Around the fire, they attempted introductions that the goblin attack had postponed. Quercucoonie spoke most openly. His homeland, Hodenbaumhaut Forest, was dying. He hoped that somewhere in Abrams Rest — or beneath it — he might find the knowledge needed to understand the cause and restore what had been lost.

Coren said he was chasing a rumour.

Lira said she was searching for something important.

Hylanku tried to explain his own purpose in broken Common. Only Lira appeared to understand him fully, but the meaning was close enough: he too had lost someone.

Luxem offered nothing.

Some stories become lighter when spoken aloud. Others merely become easier for strangers to use against you.

Nathan turned the conversation toward his own situation. He challenged Chango over the bounty and asked whether the Venaran truly intended to surrender him to the Baron. Chango did not provide the reassurance Nathan wanted.

The archaeologist warned the group about Abrams Rest. Magic there was not admired. Those who revealed it could be taken, collared and put to work. The warning came too late to undo what several people aboard the train had already witnessed, but there were few witnesses left.

Coren took first watch.

The night passed without incident.

The following day, the party encountered a woman sitting beside a campfire. Her name was Jane. She wore a brown leather jacket and hat and had been drinking long enough to make either detail difficult to appreciate.

Lira spoke with her.

The Baron's men had killed Jane's husband and son while hunting for mages. Nothing in the Blighted West had punished them for it. No guard had been tried. No debt had been recorded. The Baron's men had wanted magic, and a family had stood too close to it.

Lira stayed with Jane long enough to find whatever part of her still wanted to live. Hylanku listened, then gave the simplest judgement of the Baron anyone had yet managed.

"Baron only take."

They continued into the Rabbit Rock Mountains.

The goblin camp was easy to find.

It was also already dead.

Bodies lay throughout the camp. Whatever had happened there had been decisive enough to leave no argument about ownership of the magicite. The party searched among the remains, looking for the stolen core and for some indication of what had slaughtered the goblins before they arrived.

A statue stood within the camp.

It did not remain a statue.

The construct came to life as the party approached, moving with the certainty of a machine that required neither anger nor courage to kill. Luxem was caught by its attack and fell unconscious.

Coren and Hylanku moved to defend her.

The others joined them, striking at stone and ancient metal while the camp's previous occupants watched with empty eyes. Eventually the construct stopped moving.

The party had found the thieves.

They had also found what killed them.

Luxem did not wake to see the difference.

Session 03

Prying Out the Core

Nathan Indiana Croft asked to be unchained again.

This time he had something useful to trade.

The creature the party had destroyed was not merely a statue. Its body contained the magicite core they needed, but removing it incorrectly might damage the ore, the machine or everyone standing near it. Nathan claimed to know how the mechanism worked.

Chango loosened the archaeologist's restraints.

This did not amount to freedom. It did, however, allow Nathan to point at the construct's open chest without having to point with his chin.

The party took turns attempting to remove the magicite.

The core resisted them.

Hands slipped. Tools found no purchase. Ancient engineering proved less cooperative than dead goblins. Each failed attempt left the magicite exactly where it was and made Nathan's instructions sound slightly less impressive.

Quercucoonie finally managed it.

The core came free from the construct's chest, intact and still valuable. The Desert Rose could be made to move again.

With the immediate danger passed, the party searched the camp. Little remained worth taking. Whatever had killed the goblins had not been interested in ordinary possessions, and the goblins themselves had not owned much that survived them.

They found fresh water and refilled their skins.

In the Blighted West, this was nearly as valuable as the magicite.

Lira and Hylanku turned their attention to the creatures the goblins had left locked in cages. Raptors paced behind the bars. Freeing them offered no reward and introduced several obvious risks, so naturally the pair did it anyway.

The animals fled the camp.

The party prepared for the return journey. They had the stolen core, Nathan's knowledge and more questions than when they had left the train.

They also had Luxem.

Healing had not woken her. Neither had movement, voices or the destruction of the thing that struck her. Hylanku lifted her and carried her away from the goblin camp.

At one point, the others discussed pouring water over her.

Water was scarce, Luxem remained unconscious, and no one could confidently predict which of those facts would anger her more upon waking. They decided against it.

The group left the dead camp behind and began walking west.

The magicite was recovered.

The elf was not.

Session 04

The Elf Gate and the Setantii

On the road back to the Desert Rose, Nathan gave the dead construct a name.

It was a Setantii: a metal guardian from the Old World, built before the Great Devastation and powered by magicite. Whatever purpose it had once served was no longer recorded in anything the party carried with them. A thousand years later, it had been left in a goblin camp to continue obeying instructions that no one remembered giving.

Nathan knew more about the ancient world than he had first admitted.

He told the party that Baron Maxim Von Basten believed valuable technology remained buried in the Drop beneath Abrams Rest. The Baron's miners recovered magicite, ruins and relics from the depths. Professor Godt was said to make old devices work again — or find new uses for them.

The distinction was unlikely to comfort anyone used as part of the process.

The conversation returned repeatedly to the Baron. Jane's family, Nathan's bounty, the enslavement of mages and the dangers beneath Abrams Rest all appeared to end at the same name.

The party also learned more of the city awaiting them. The Colosseum stood in the western quarter and provided one of Abrams Rest's few public entertainments. Its fights were to the death.

In a place where ordinary life was already so efficient at killing people, the Baron had still found a way to sell tickets.

Later, the party discovered a carved archway set into a crevice.

Nathan identified it as an Aiudara — an elven gate. Long ago, the elves had used such gateways to leave the Astlands and return to their own world. Most of the gates were now depowered.

This one was no exception.

The arch stood silent, framing stone and shadow instead of another planet. Lira regarded it as a remnant of her people's history. Luxem remained unconscious nearby, unable or unwilling to offer an opinion.

Whatever road the elves had once taken was closed.

The dead still found theirs.

Four skeletons and an armed captain rose against the party. Chango's shots shattered bone. Coren moved among them with his blade. Lira fought beside the others, and Hylanku placed himself between the attackers and anyone more vulnerable.

The minotaur took wound after wound and remained standing by very little.

When the last skeleton fell, the party made camp.

Coren took first watch.

Somewhere in the dark, he began to hear whispers.

He did not wake the others. He did not explain the words. By morning, the road remained where it had been, the Aiudara remained dead, and Coren behaved as though nothing had spoken to him.

The party continued toward the train.

Behind them, the gate led nowhere.

Session 05

Arrival, and the Bats Over the Colosseum

The party returned to the Desert Rose the following morning.

Thistogg had spent their absence clearing the train. The dead passengers had been removed and buried with more respect than the goblins had shown them. He seemed genuinely surprised to see the party return alive.

The recovered magicite core was installed, and the engine woke.

While the others prepared to leave, Chango cleaned and reloaded his pistols. He spoke privately with Nathan, though the archaeologist's exact status remained unclear. He was no longer fully bound, but neither had Chango declared the bounty abandoned.

Quercucoonie played with rocks.

Lira worked with dirt and plants.

Luxem remained still.

The survivors boarded a first-class carriage and resumed their journey. Chango discovered a pocket watch engraved with the initials T.R. No one recognised them.

Nathan repeated his warning as the train approached Abrams Rest: do not show magic. The Baron enslaved those who possessed it, and collars ensured that a mage could use only whatever spell their owner required.

The city rose around a ravine, guarded by walls and watchtowers.

Outside the gates, refugees begged to be admitted.

The guards threatened them with rifles.

Inside, the party was directed toward the Hexes and met by Phil Philliamson, an escort paid by a benefactor he refused to name. A scar crossed Phil's face. When magic was mentioned, it shone blue and produced faint whispers. He claimed that magic had saved his life and left him with the reputation of a marksman called Crosswind Phil.

He guided the group to the Dancing Cat.

Along the way they passed food stalls, cages and the ordinary business of a city that sold survival in portions. Quercucoonie bought a live rhizan. Lira was less interested in the merchandise than in the animals still trapped behind bars.

At the Dancing Cat, Pok the tengu barman had heard nothing about a captured mage. Hylanku and Lira arranged a room for Luxem and sought a doctor from the Cliffs.

Then Ressa Vann arrived.

Coren knew her.

Years before, Ressa had recruited him into the Silent Knives, the resistance movement that had tried to remove the Baron. Now she worked as one of his foremen. The Baron had spared her when most of the Knives were wiped out.

Ressa believed Coren might have betrayed them.

Coren asked about Jareth. He had believed his former partner dead, stabbed through the neck in the safehouse on the night Coren fled Abrams Rest.

Ressa had never heard that Jareth died.

The possibility opened quietly and refused to close.

Rumours spoke of Professor Godt, the mind behind the Baron's rule. Creatures, relics and people taken from the Drop were said to disappear into the Professor's library in the Cobbles. Pok confirmed the location. Ressa agreed to seek work and passes for the party.

The following day, Quercucoonie visited a Venaran blacksmith named Yangu and bought a crossbow. Chango learned that Yangu was trapped by debt to the Baron and tried to persuade him to accept help. Yangu showed little interest in rescue. Hope could be expensive in Abrams Rest, particularly when offered by strangers.

A raven landed on Chango when he left the smithy.

Phil eventually admitted that he wanted the Baron overthrown, though he still would not name his benefactor. Coren scouted the Cobbles gates and memorised the guards' positions.

Lira then revealed why she needed the Colosseum.

Thallion was there.

Her elk had been captured and marked for Professor Godt. Luddy, the pitmaster, would release him only if Lira and her companions fought in the arena.

The group registered under the name Abrams Unrest.

They faced five returning champions.

Hylanku used a boulder to kill a halfling opponent, concealing the magic involved as best he could. An orc warrior brought him down. Coren dropped the orc, only for it to rise again. Phil killed three champions, including the orc, while the others survived by increasingly narrow margins.

When the fight ended, Quercucoonie gave the crowd a victory speech loud and theatrical enough to distract them while Lira revived Hylanku.

The Colosseum honoured its bargain.

Thallion was released into the arena and ran to Lira.

For a moment, something lost had been returned.

Then giant bats descended from the sky.

They tore into the Colosseum and the spectators. Chango fell. Quercucoonie was knocked unconscious. Lira went down during the retreat. Hylanku lifted companions from the ground and carried them toward the gates. Thallion dragged Chango and later bore Lira.

Above them circled a red-skinned figure in horned armour, mounted on a giant white bat.

"For too long you have invaded our domain."

Coren held one gate while the others escaped. Phil held the opposite side and shot a bat from the air before fleeing. Hylanku carried Chango and Quercucoonie. Thallion carried Lira. Coren shut the gate behind them.

The party escaped the arena.

Abrams Rest did not.

Session 06

The Masked Healer and the Warning from Nar-Voth

The flight from the Colosseum continued through streets filled with wings, screams and falling debris.

Hylanku used magic to draw the bats away from the others. Coren climbed onto the rooftops and made himself visible enough to become another target. Thallion threw a barrel into the chaos, proving that an elk required neither hands nor restraint to contribute.

Before leaving the roofs, Coren looked back at the rider.

The figure wore horned armour and carried a trident. The white bat had landed upon a tower near the centre of the city.

The party reached the Dancing Cat and called for Ressa and Pok to let them inside.

Yangu was already sheltering there. So were many of the wounded. Moving among them was a figure in a long coat, a beaked mask and goggles — the doctor brought from the Cliffs.

The healer used magic openly.

No runic collar circled his neck. Light or power passed directly from his body, closing wounds and restoring the injured. Quercucoonie regained consciousness under the spell, then imitated what he had seen and reproduced the magic himself.

Hylanku questioned the doctor about his freedom. In Abrams Rest, anyone capable of such healing should have been owned.

The doctor offered no useful explanation.

Chango, newly restored and still carrying the shock of the escape, said he needed a break.

Thallion pressed his forehead to Lira's and licked her. Whatever else Abrams Rest had taken, it had not managed to keep them apart.

Ressa questioned the group about the attackers. She believed the bats must have come from the Drop. Hylanku suggested the Darklands. Coren reported that the creatures had been carrying prisoners downward.

Ressa provided deliberately vague work permits and a single gate pass into the Cobbles. It was not enough for the entire party, but it was more than they had possessed before the attack.

She warned Lira to hide Thallion.

Lira answered that no one would take him again.

Coren asked Ressa about the Silent Knives. She took him into her office, opened a bottle and listened while he revealed more of the truth.

On the night of the failed coup, Coren had carried out the assassination of one of the Baron's lieutenants alone. Jareth had failed to appear because he had become consumed by a spell. Coren returned to the safehouse, found him there and stabbed him before fleeing.

He still did not explain everything.

Coren asked what Ressa knew of the marsh. She dismissed such things as fairytales. Coren told her they were not.

Ressa gave him a final warning. If she learned that he had helped destroy the Silent Knives, she would kill him.

Coren told her that, should that day come, he would hand her the blade himself.

Elsewhere in the tavern, Hylanku asked Yangu for armour. He had failed to protect the others as completely as he believed he should. Yangu promised to find something.

Quercucoonie fed a seed to the live rhizan he had bought in the Hexes. The creature became somewhat less aggressive. Verdant Shade, which had vanished when Quercucoonie fell unconscious, later reformed from a twisting mass of roots.

Lira attempted to read the future and learn what the bats would do next.

The fortune gave no answer.

The rider did.

His voice carried across Abrams Rest from outside the tavern.

"Take this as warning. Do not enter Nar-Voth again."

Lira recognised the name. Nar-Voth was a region within the Darklands, far below the surface.

The bats began to withdraw.

During the attack, Nathan had defended the room where Luxem lay, striking at a bat that came through one of the windows. The masked healer examined her again. She appeared physically unharmed.

He could not explain why she remained unconscious.

Yangu returned with a worn but sturdy suit of dull half plate and gave it to Hylanku. Ressa supplied the party with healing potions, potency runes and crossbow bolts. Hylanku received a healer's kit from the masked doctor.

Later, Hylanku sat alone with the emblem Old Bull had given him. He prayed to Jarak, lamenting that he had fallen in the Colosseum and failed to protect his companions.

Something answered.

Hylanku emerged from the prayer able to channel healing magic of his own.

The group invited Nathan to accompany them into the Cobbles. His knowledge of the Old World might be useful near the Professor's library. Nathan warned that Verdant Shade would attract attention. Quercucoonie did not appear troubled by this.

Lira solved the problem of hiding Thallion by casting a spell. The elk leapt into her hat and vanished. Antlers appeared upon its brim.

When the party finally stepped outside, they saw what the bat attack had done.

Bodies covered the streets. The Hexes had been smashed open. Shopkeepers fought looters among ruined stalls. Survivors cried over the dead.

There were no dead guards among them.

Session 07

Into the Cobbles

The party returned to the station beneath the Cobbles and watched the gate.

Guards stood openly around the entrance. Quercucoonie cast a spell to determine whether more were hidden nearby.

One of the visible guards noticed.

A shot struck the ground in front of Quercucoonie.

A short, broad guard with a handlebar moustache demanded an explanation before deciding whether to aim the next shot higher. Quercucoonie claimed the group had permits to help repair the Cobbles and affected fear over the recent bat attack. Coren invoked Ressa's name and offered the party as labourers.

The story worked.

The guards assigned them to Gaskin, a tall and gaunt man charged with ensuring they went where they were told. He signalled for the gate to open.

Great metal cogs turned.

The party entered the Cobbles.

Compared with the lower city, it resembled an attempt to remember the world before the Devastation. Its stonework was clean. Trees grew in gardens. Water moved through fountains while people beyond the gates measured each drink.

The bats had damaged this quarter too.

Gaskin took the party to a garden and ordered them to move bodies. Enslaved workers repaired the damage under guard.

Hylanku searched every face for Foxfire.

He found instead a black-haired human woman in a rusted runic collar, using magic to mend a tree. She wore a woollen sack. The collar's runes shone as her fingers moved.

Hylanku asked where the mages lived.

She pointed toward the Damn and toward houses northwest of the library. She had not seen a red-haired elf. When Hylanku introduced himself and asked for her name, the woman gave the only answer Abrams Rest had left her.

"They took it."

Quercucoonie spoke to another labourer cleaning blood from the stones. She knew of the Professor but had not seen him recently. No one was permitted near the library without approval.

Coren remembered an old civil library in the western Cobbles. The location matched the rumours surrounding Professor Godt.

While moving bodies, Coren found a pass upon a dead nobleman.

The nobleman no longer required it.

Coren did.

After the garden, Gaskin offered the party further work. They could assist with damaged warehouses or enter the Riad, a bathhouse sealed by its own defence system after a bat became trapped inside.

They chose the bathhouse.

Outside the Riad stood several guards and a woman dressed in silk, jewellery and thigh-high boots. A face covering concealed part of her expression. A headdress with two blue tassels rose above it. She carried a large rifle.

The woman refused to enter the bathhouse herself. She threatened the guards with her father's displeasure if they failed to solve the problem before the end of the day.

Her father was Baron Maxim Von Basten.

The woman was Selene.

She inspected Hylanku and wondered aloud whether she wanted him for dinner or for something else. She looked at Quercucoonie and called him a starter.

It was not clear which interpretation offered greater safety.

Gaskin proposed sending the party inside. Selene left the consequences to them.

Quercucoonie suggested that smaller bats might have entered through the drains. He then prayed to Orth for deliverance from the tyranny of man and beast.

Coren picked the lock.

Hylanku entered first.

Five clay figures wandered through the bathhouse. The Riad's defence protocol considered the party intruders and attacked.

Hylanku raised a protective shield over the group. The others broke the constructs apart piece by piece. When the final homunculus fell, Verdant Shade tore an aquamarine gemstone from its eye.

The bathhouse remained sealed.

Somewhere deeper inside, the bat was still alive.

Session 08

The Riad and the Old Safehouse

Quercucoonie searched the roots beneath the Riad for signs of the bat.

He found none.

The party continued into the bathhouse.

Hylanku discovered two bodies in one of the pools. The water was boiling. One corpse bore a vast wound across its back. The other had no visible injury, though its robes had been torn.

Its gloves remained perfectly white.

Coren pulled the body from the water and removed them. The cloth reached beyond the wrists and appeared untouched by the heat, blood and violence around them. The party suspected the gloves were magical.

The dead might once have been priests. The room and pools suggested some form of baptism or ritual purification. Whatever holiness the Riad claimed had not prevented the bat from feeding there.

A leaking pipe had weakened part of the floor. Quercucoonie nearly fell through it, then used the opening deliberately. In a smaller, raccoon-dog-like form, he entered the tunnel beneath the bathhouse.

Tracks ran through it, sized for the clay guardians.

At the far end, Quercucoonie reached a panel and looked into another chamber. Two armoured figures stood beyond it holding glaives.

The armour appeared empty.

He returned to the others.

Coren opened one of the locked doors above. Two more bodies lay inside, both marked by scratches. He searched for passes but found only a dark blue potion. The party identified it as a potion of water breathing.

Beyond another locked door was a pool containing three more bodies. The party threw a corpse ahead of them as bait.

Nothing moved.

In the next chamber, they found the living.

Two adults and a child huddled together in the water.

The bat descended upon Hylanku.

Coren stabbed it. Quercucoonie tore at it with a transformed claw. Chango climbed a pillar for a clear shot, struck the creature once and missed with the second pistol.

Hylanku ended the fight.

He caught the bat with his horns, drove through its chin and threw it into the pool.

The family survived.

In return, the party asked for their Cobbles passes. Gratitude and recent terror made negotiation brief. The group now possessed four passes.

They removed the bat's head and carried it outside.

When the severed head landed at the guards' feet, the Riad's defence system finally disengaged. Gaskin left to report the party's success.

The guards had been watching them since the Desert Rose arrived. They had recovered the train's core, survived the Colosseum and now cleared the bathhouse. This made them useful enough to tolerate.

They were given permission to remain in the Cobbles and assigned a third-floor flat.

Coren knew the building.

Five years earlier, it had been a Silent Knives safehouse. He had lived there with Jareth. It was also the place where he had stabbed him.

The interior had changed, but one chest of drawers remained. Coren remembered the hidden compartment inside it.

There he found a charcoal drawing of himself.

He searched the entry hall for traces of Jareth's spell. Only a faint outline remained where the wood had lightened around the old circle.

The others saw marks on the floor.

Coren saw the last place he had left someone alive enough to haunt him.

The party planned to scout the Professor's library the following day.

Coren attempted it alone that night.

He climbed onto the roofs, disturbed a patrol and made enough noise to draw the guards' attention. A shot struck him before he escaped back to the safehouse.

Hylanku healed the wound.

Coren agreed that the next attempt should involve the others.

The white gloves taken from the Riad were identified as healer's gloves. The party gave them to Lira.

Morning approached over the Cobbles.

The library remained closed.

One window, however, would not remain so for long.

Session 09

Auctions, Funerals and the Dead Upstairs

Abrams Rest attempted to behave as though the attack had ended.

Markets reopened. Workers returned to their stations. The dead were moved somewhere less disruptive to business. In the Cobbles, the fountains continued to run.

The party separated for the day and agreed to meet that evening at the Magic Carpet.

Quercucoonie visited the Galleries, where the city's better goods were reserved for those permitted above the gates. At Larriot's Little Luxuries, he sold the aquamarine taken from the Riad for fifty-five gold.

Nearby, an auction was underway.

The goods were people.

One man stood six feet tall, muscular, with a black mohawk that continued into a plaited ponytail and a heavy black beard. The auctioneer advertised him for labour or combat in the Colosseum.

He was listed as Auction Item 297.

Quercucoonie entered the bidding and bought him for sixty gold.

While completing the paperwork, Quercucoonie asked for the man's name. The seller explained that names were not recorded because owners usually preferred to assign their own. The slaves, he was told, were not people.

Quercucoonie wrote Little Freeman.

The man wore a collar and manacles. Quercucoonie was given a wand containing a command spell with which to control him.

Instead, he spoke to him.

Quercucoonie explained that he had purchased him because he looked sad and that he did not wish to own anyone. The man gave his real name as Kuldosk. Quercucoonie offered him freedom in return for working off the price and invited him to dinner.

As they walked away, Kuldosk muttered the only reasonable response.

"What the fuck is happening?"

Lira passed the Desert Rose and found a makeshift church filled with the bodies of those killed in the attack. Incense failed to hide the smell of blood.

She offered to help.

Alongside a gnomish woman, Lira wrapped the dead in cloth for the funeral rites of Abrams Rest. While she worked, she spoke carefully with the mourners. The Baron's guards had allowed the city to die. There had once been an understanding that servitude purchased safety. The bat attack had exposed the lie.

People listened.

They remembered what had happened to the Silent Knives and understood that such thoughts could be fatal. Even so, Lira told them that they were not alone and that the party would help where it could.

A young man asked to touch Thallion. The elk looked to Lira before allowing it.

Trust, unlike obedience, required permission.

Hylanku searched the Galleries for Foxfire.

A servant waiting outside a boutique remembered a red-haired mage from an earlier auction. The woman had been purchased by someone close to the Baron — a person with short brown hair, brown eyes and a cigarette always in hand.

It was not enough to find Foxfire.

It was enough to know she might still be alive.

Hylanku visited Yangu. The blacksmith was making collars.

He looked at Hylanku with such sadness that he told him to speak with Ressa.

Before leaving, Hylanku asked Yangu to break the chain between his manacles. It took four strikes. Hylanku kept the iron around his wrists.

At the Dancing Cat, Ressa took him upstairs.

Luxem lay beneath a sheet.

She had died on the night the others entered the Cobbles.

Hylanku uncovered her face and asked whether someone had killed her. Ressa said the doctor found no sign of violence. He had suggested a bleed within the brain, though simple healing magic should have repaired it.

The word dead was used because the living require a word that ends the conversation.

Hylanku said Luxem had been hard, but good.

He placed Old Bull's medallion upon her chest and asked Jarak to care for her spirit. For a moment, he believed the emblem answered.

Ressa had kept Luxem's possessions instead of allowing them to be taken. Among them was a fine bottle of clear Iron Wine. Hylanku took it. The remaining equipment was left for some future traveller who might need it more.

Ressa, Pok and Hylanku poured whisky.

They drank to the next part of Luxem's journey.

A mass funeral was being arranged for the victims of the attack. The bodies would be wrapped, placed upon the water and carried over the falls into the Drop. Hylanku asked for Luxem to be included.

Afterward he returned to Yangu's smithy and worked in silence.

Coren spent his day among the older merchants of the Galleries, searching for anyone who remembered the failed coup.

A cobbler did.

On the night the Silent Knives fell, guards had carried Jareth's body from the safehouse. A man with long silver hair and a scar upon his cheek intervened. He ordered the body taken toward the Professor's library.

The man was Lorcend Von Basten.

The cobbler confirmed what the city whispered: people taken into the library were rarely seen again.

Coren inspected the building in daylight. It resembled a mansion pretending to be an ancient civic building, with ornate glass, bronze doors and a control panel beside the entrance. Electricity ran within, likely powered by magicite.

The shutters were closed.

One upper window had been left open.

By evening, each member of the party carried something new toward the Magic Carpet.

A freed man.

A rumour.

A route inside.

And news that could not be made lighter by sharing it.

Session 10

Hylanku's Story and the Stairway Below

The party gathered at the Magic Carpet.

Chango had arrived first. Quercucoonie introduced Kuldosk and explained that he had traded a rock for a friend. This was not an entirely faithful account of the auction, but it was kinder than the paperwork.

Hylanku bought six whiskies. The barwoman filled the glasses with magic. Lira brought water for Thallion.

Once everyone was seated, Hylanku told them about Luxem.

She had died in her sleep. He had asked that her body be wrapped with the others and carried into the Drop according to the rites of Abrams Rest.

Lira had spent the day preparing the dead. She said she would help wrap Luxem too. Among elves, she explained, the dead were sometimes buried with something useful for the next world.

Quercucoonie offered one of his seeds.

Wherever the road led, Luxem would at least have the means to grow food.

Coren shared what he had learned about Jareth and the library window. Hylanku questioned whether such plans should be discussed in front of Kuldosk.

Quercucoonie removed Kuldosk's claim of ownership completely.

Kuldosk agreed to help them enter the library.

Hylanku told the others what he had learned about Foxfire. Coren described Lorcend Von Basten: silver hair, a scar on his cheek, the Baron's son.

Hylanku knew the description.

Lorcend was the man who had killed Old Bull and shattered Hylanku's horn.

For the first time, Hylanku told the entire story.

Years before, a caravan crossed the Blighted West carrying people of many races and faiths. They survived by moving together. Among them was Foxfire, a young red-haired half-elven mage who used her abilities to provide food and water while avoiding the Baron's collars.

Old Bull guided the caravan.

He was an elderly minotaur, devoted to Jarak, the six-horned first minotaur. He understood the earth and its spirits and possessed a talent for leading the caravan away from danger.

At the Coccium Ruins, the travellers found Hylanku.

He was emaciated, half-feral and unable to remember anything before the ruins. The others wanted to leave him. Old Bull approached instead.

He calmed Hylanku, spoke to him and persuaded the caravan to take him in.

Over the following years, Old Bull taught Hylanku about Jarak, minotaur history and the Petran nature of their people. Hylanku grew strong enough to protect the caravan that had saved him. Foxfire became his friend.

The caravan survived partly through the food and water Foxfire created.

Eventually, the Baron's men heard of her.

They attacked.

Old Bull stood against them while Hylanku tried to protect Foxfire and the others. The Baron's silver-haired enforcer beat Old Bull to his knees.

Hylanku attempted to intervene.

The enforcer fired.

The bullet shattered Hylanku's left horn and struck Old Bull in the chest.

Old Bull died.

Hylanku was chained before he could learn whether Foxfire escaped. The Baron's men sent him to the mines near Inis Town, where his strength and instinct for the earth allowed him to travel deeper than other workers. He protected fellow slaves, found lost miners and survived passages that should have killed him.

Far below the surface, Hylanku discovered magicite.

The emblem of Jarak responded to it.

When the Baron's men saw what Hylanku could recover, they sent him to Abrams Rest for work in the Drop.

Now the name of Old Bull's killer had returned.

Lorcend Von Basten.

The party waited for darkness.

Lira placed Thallion within her hat. Quercucoonie used the roots beneath the ground to determine whether anyone waited near Coren's open window.

The way appeared clear.

Coren climbed first. His foot slipped, but he reached the window. Verdant Shade threw Quercucoonie upward while the Tenuki was in a smaller animal form.

Lira missed Coren's hand, caught the ledge and pulled herself up.

Hylanku climbed without difficulty.

Chango did not.

Hylanku failed to catch him, so Coren lowered a rope. Chango reached the window slowly. Kuldosk climbed after him with far less effort.

The party entered the library.

They separated among the shelves.

Quercucoonie searched for books concerning nature and the death of his homeland. He found a record of the southeast from before the Great Devastation. It suggested that the Tenuki of that age had known what was coming and performed a mass ritual to preserve their land.

The book did not contain the spell.

It did, however, suggest that the Blight might be pushed back if the lost ritual could be recovered.

Coren found a pedestal holding a book and a device bearing nine numbered positions.

Lira found records concerning an experiment called Subject 001.

Chango found histories and another journal.

Hylanku found handwritten experiment notes.

Coren discovered the record of Subject 442.

It described the morning after the Silent Knives' failed coup. The Professor had sensed hag magic near the safehouse. Lorcend brought him the body of a young man stabbed through the neck.

The man still lived.

Jareth had been cursed by a green hag named Callow May. He would never die, but neither would he heal. The hag described Jareth as the lover of her son and allowed the Professor to do whatever he wished with him.

Callow May was Coren's mother.

Further entries described Jareth being altered piece by piece, sections of his body replaced with parts from larger creatures and dragon hide.

Other journals described older subjects.

Subject 001 had once been a young woman. The Professor transformed her in an attempt to create an obedient servant. What remained was a suffering, amorphous creature that healed almost instantly from any injury.

Subject 126 had been a young ape taken from the Nanji River. Repeated treatments increased his intelligence, changed his body and taught him to use weapons. Before leaving during the Great Devastation, the Professor placed the subject in cryogenic stasis.

The final entry considered giving him a name.

Subject 469 had been an elderly minotaur brought to the Professor by Lorcend. The body carried an emblem of the Horns of Bos and an old war axe.

The Professor turned the skeleton into a guardian.

He removed muscle and one horn for use in another subject.

Hylanku understood whose body it had been.

Old Bull had reached the library before him.

Among the shelves, Quercucoonie noticed something hiding.

The creature was almost humanoid. Its body appeared gelatinous, its skin melting from it. It could make only one sound.

"Ort."

Lira called the creature she, and it became less determined to hide. Quercucoonie asked whether she was a prisoner. She shook her head. He asked whether they could help her leave.

She retreated.

Lira offered to ease her pain, but the creature recoiled from her hand.

A journal described failed experiments being placed upon a pile. Quercucoonie asked whether the creature had once been a gnome.

"Ort," she answered.

Elsewhere, Quercucoonie discovered a chamber containing a chained statue and candles. Something about it unsettled him. The sound the creature repeated was too close to Orth, the god to whom Quercucoonie prayed.

A chest contained adder's poison and a medallion marked with a horn and sword.

The journals identified it as the symbol of the Horns of Bos, royal protectors of the lost Minotaur Empire.

The numbered device remained locked.

Hylanku examined the records and recognised a pattern. The Professor considered only certain experiments successful.

Subject 126.

Subject 442.

Subject 469.

The party entered the numbers.

The mechanism moved.

The floor opened before them, revealing a concealed stairway descending beneath the library.

Above them stood shelves filled with the Professor's recorded cruelties.

Below waited the work he had chosen not to record where visitors might find it.

The party gathered at the top of the stairs.

Then they looked down.
