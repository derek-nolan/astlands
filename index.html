A Thousand Years After the Great Devastation

Water is scarce.
Magic is outlawed.
The Baron owns everything else.

In the rock-diseased desert called the Blighted West, one city still stands: Abrams Rest, the last clean water in a poisoned land, and the iron fist of Baron Maxim Von Basten that squeezes it. This is the chronicle of the strangers who rode in on the Desert Rose — and the unrest they've caused ever since.

The Story So Far

The Desert Rose was two days out from Abrams Rest when the goblins came through the windows. Six of them, screaming, and the train's passengers — most of them strangers to one another — became a party in the space of a single fight. Chango K Caza, a Venaran bounty hunter, had his prisoner in tow: the archaeologist Nathan Indiana Croft, chained but talking. Coren Vael, a rogue with one blue eye and one green, said little. Hylanku Lonehorn, a minotaur who spoke like the words cost him something, put himself between the goblins and the others without being asked. Lira Cervan, an elf druid hunting for her lost elk, and Luxem, a sorcerer who wanted to talk about none of it, fought alongside Quercucoonie, a Tenuki summoner whose eidolon, Verdant Shade, ended the fight by turning a goblin into paste with one blow.

When it was over, the train's engineer Thistogg gave them the bad news: everyone else aboard was dead, and the goblins had stolen the magicite core that powered the engine. Nathan pointed them toward a temple that might hold raw magicite and the promise of treasure. The party chose to hunt the thieves instead.

They tracked the goblins into the Rabbit Rock Mountains, camped a night that loosened tongues — Quercucoonie's dying forest, Coren's rumour, Lira's something-lost, Hylanku's someone-lost — and walked into a goblin camp already dead. What killed it woke up when they got close: a stone guardian, ancient and merciless, that put Luxem down before Coren and Hylanku drove it back. Nathan, freed just enough to be useful, told them what they'd actually fought — a Setantii, a relic of the Old World — and helped them pry the magicite from its chest.

"Too long have we invaded their domain."

Recharged and rolling again, the Desert Rose carried them into Abrams Rest past refugees begging at the gates and guards answering with rifles. An escort named Phil Philliamson — paid by someone he wouldn't name — steered them through the Hexes to the Dancing Cat, where the foreman Ressa Vann offered work, and where Coren learned that the resistance cell he'd once belonged to, the Silent Knives, had been wiped out in his absence. He'd assumed his old partner Jareth was dead by his own hand. Now he wasn't sure — and every thread pointed toward a locked library in the Cobbles, the mansion-laboratory of a thing the whole city called simply the Professor.

They earned a way in the hard way: fighting under the banner of Abrams Unrest in the Colosseum to win back Lira's elk, Thallion, from the pens where the Professor's next subjects were kept. They won the match. Then the sky came apart. Giant bats fell on the arena and the city beyond it, led by a horned rider on white wings who screamed a warning about a domain nobody in Abrams Rest remembered invading. The party got out. Not everyone who helped them did.

Cobbles work-passes, a masked healer with no collar and no explanation, a warning shouted from the rooftops in a language older than the Baron's rule — the party pieced together enough to talk their way past the Cobbles guards as disaster labourers. They cleared a bathhouse of animated gargoyles, pulled a family out from under a feral bat, and were rewarded with a flat on the third floor of a safehouse Coren recognised immediately: the one he'd fled five years ago, blood on the floor where he'd left Jareth for dead.

What followed was worse than nostalgia. A journal, Subject 442, described a boy stabbed through the neck who wouldn't die — cursed, not saved, by a hag who turned out to be Coren's own mother. Another journal, Subject 126, described an ape-like prisoner being remade piece by piece into something else entirely, right up until the day the mages came looking and the Professor put the subject into cryogenic stasis rather than lose him. Hylanku learned, elsewhere in the city, exactly how he'd lost Old Bull and Foxfire to a silver-haired enforcer with a rifle. Everything the party has found — the auctions, the collars, the pile of failed experiments in a locked room — points to the same building, and the same set of locked drawers.

Ten sessions in, the party has climbed through a library window in the dark, cracked a three-number lock built from the Professor's own case files, and watched the floor open beneath them onto a stairwell no one built for guests.
